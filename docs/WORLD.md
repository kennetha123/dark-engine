# The World

Locked truth for the setting. What the world **is**, as currently settled.

Why it says what it says lives in `journals/world/`. The people in it live in `docs/cast/`.
Engine scope and architecture are `docs/PLAN.md`, and nothing here changes them.

---

## The shape of it

A country of some twenty-five settled places, a day's walk apart. A year is 365 days. Crossing
the country costs real days out of that year.

Four hundred years ago humans and undead fought the first war. The people who remember it
first-hand are all undead, because humans do not live that long. Humanity has three generations
and a legend; the undead have a memory.

## Three territories

**Human** — Ravingard, Sovern, Mariacth, Bosreim, Eidis, Livingard, Pincaoth, Surreanoth, Green
Town, Gulard, Mirkish, Folkstagaard, Centreau, Tarniard, Velkstein, School of Mage Tower, the
forge, the veterans' village.

**Neutral** — Birkstein, Erdlis, Narimbia, High Fall, Magnoria.

**Undead** — Porchin, Rinkart, Castle of Remorse, Castle of Horror, Dark Lord Castle.

The colours move. Undead can take territory, and a war can be started deliberately.

Velkstein and Rinkart face each other across the water — *defense of humanity* and *defense of
undead*, dug in as mirrors.

**Neutral ground is the only place the sides meet.** Undead cannot enter human towns, so every
mixed trade, every rumour crossing the line, and every co-op party that does not share an
allegiance passes through those five places. Birkstein is nearest the human heartland and carries
the most of it — fitting, for a city of poverty and thieves that does not care what anybody is.

Each neutral place holds a power that belongs to no side.

## What you are

Character creation asks about a life, not a class: **station** (noble, common, poor, lowborn, elf,
undead) crossed with **leaning** (politics, knighthood, study, magic, trade, freedom, leisure,
vengeance, faith, craft).

**The answers decide where you wake.** There is no single starting town. See
`journals/world/01` for the full table.

**Background is a head start, not a cage.** Roles are gated on stats, not on class. Anyone can
train into anything; being born to it only means you begin past the line.

## Allegiance

Set at creation, never fixed there. Two people can change it, and both are mortal:

- **Theresa**, Mother Guardian of the church — cleanses an undead back into a living human.
- **Ambrose**, the Necrolord — turns a human undead.

Leave one and come back and they are not angry at a state change, they are angry at **you**. The
fight starts.

**If either dies, nobody can change allegiance again. Ever, for everyone.** The seats do not
refill, so the world narrows across the year: on day 10 anything is possible, and by day 300
doors have closed — some by your hand, some by a friend's.

### Being undead

No hunger, no thirst, no cold. Five of the eight hotbar slots go quiet. Low undead rot; high
undead do not, and climbing out of the rot is the dark path's ladder.

Human towns are hostile. An undead lives in undead territory or on neutral ground.

**Easier to survive, harder to live.**

## The powers

**The Dark Lord** is the strongest thing in the world — reality-mending, magic, dark power and
immense physical strength together. He is not evil. He is **bored**, having fought everything
worth fighting, and too lazy for politics. His two guardians run the faction between them.

**Garskin**, second guardian, has the highest physical strength in the world — above the Dark
Lord's. He wants peace, gives money to whoever needs it, helps whoever is in front of him, and
does not distinguish undead from human. He wants a flower garden. He keeps a four-hundred-year-old
living dog in his basement, unrotting because he takes its decay into himself.

**Ambrose**, the Necrolord, was the greatest human who ever lived — a bishop and a Mage Tower
scholar. A century ago paladins murdered his bride on a false accusation that somebody arranged,
to drive him out of his life and into the land of the undead, where a low-ranking vampire bit him.
He is now the greatest vampire there has ever been, and he cannot beat Garskin.

Everyone who wronged him died of old age. He has been at war with an institution for a hundred
years, and there is nobody left to kill.

**Gandahl** sits idle at High Fall. Anti-magic, a match for Garskin physically, stronger than the
Dark Lord. The most powerful ally in the game and the worst enemy in it. He asks one thing: land a
single hit on him.

## Two factions, each split

| | War | Peace |
|---|---|---|
| **Undead** | Ambrose — a century-old grief and a real injustice | Garskin, who cannot be beaten and would rather garden |
| **Humanity** | Paladins rotting quietly under Paul Gardner | Theresa *(proposed — see `journals/world/02`)* |

The paladins' crime is a **century old**. Paul Gardner was not born. He is stoic, fixed on
straight justice, and does not look at his own people — which is exactly why they can be corrupt
beneath him.

## How the year ends

| Ending | Reached by |
|---|---|
| **Your own death** | Dying. Your death is the story |
| **The hero's death** | Taking the dark religion and killing Rein |
| **The Dark Lord's death** | Taking humanity and killing him |
| **The year runs out** | Staying neutral for all 365 days |

The Dark Lord can be reached on day one. Four Relics of the Gods kill him — **sword, runes,
hammer, book** — and that is how mortals do it. But the **Sword of Saint** lies unguarded in
Castle of Horror, and one relic with enough skill is enough. He left it there. A bored god does
not misplace the weapon that kills him.

Stay out of the war for the whole year and the hero party gathers the relics and finishes it
themselves. Neutrality is the ending where **the world saves itself and you watched**.

## Death

**Single player: death ends the run.** One life, one year. The save becomes a chronicle.

**Co-op: the year continues.** A new character is made and wakes where their own background says.

A body **stays where it falls**, with everything it carried. Settlements bury their dead — the
church does it, a sexton in every town. The wild does not, so the roads become a record of
everything that has gone wrong.

**An unburied body can be raised.** That is what gives burial its weight, and it applies to the
hero party as much as to your friends.

## The authorial rule

Every reward the player takes is paid for by somebody else, and they usually find out afterwards.

- Kill Garskin — the dog rots, Ambrose is unleashed, the war begins.
- Take the relics — Rein's party goes anyway, and dies.
- Take a place in that party — Shanya never becomes archmage, and never marries him.
- Kill Theresa or Ambrose — a door closes for everyone, forever.

This is one voice, not four ideas. Apply it on purpose.
