# Journal world/02 — The two who can change what you are

Status: Decided.
Date: 2026-09-25. Depends on: `journals/world/00`, `journals/world/01`.

## Goal

Settle allegiance: what being undead costs and grants, how a character crosses between the two
sides, and what closes that door permanently.

## Decisions

**Allegiance is fluid.** It is set at creation but never fixed there. Fixed allegiance makes a
background into a class; fluid allegiance makes it a past. An undead maid in the Dark Lord's
castle who decides to help humanity is the better story, and the 365-day year gives room to change
your mind twice.

**Two people can change it, and they are mortal.**

- **Theresa**, the Mother Guardian of the church — cleanses an undead and returns them to life as
  a human.
- **Ambrose**, the Necrolord — turns a human undead.

**Going back to the one you left enrages them.** Turn undead through Ambrose, return to Theresa,
then come back to Ambrose again: he is not angry at a state change, he is angry at **you**, and
the fight starts. He remembers. The same holds on the other side.

**If either dies, allegiance can no longer be changed.** That is permanent for the whole world,
including for other players.

**That makes it a co-op weapon with no PvP attached.** Four players; one goes dark and kills
Theresa; he has permanently denied his three friends any way back to humanity. World state does
the damage, not a duel.

**It must be telegraphed.** If a player does not understand what killing her means before they
swing, it reads as arbitrary rather than as tragedy. Someone warns them — a sexton, a paladin,
anyone.

**The seats do not refill.** So the world narrows across the year: on day 10 everything is
possible; by day 300 doors have closed, some by your hand, some by a friend's. That arc emerges
from the system rather than from a script, and it is the shape a 365-day structure wants.

### What being undead does

**The survival game switches off.** No hunger, no thirst, no cold. Food, drink, cloak, tent and
firewood stop mattering — five of the eight hotbar slots go quiet.

**Low undead rot. High undead do not.** Rot is the beginner's tax, and climbing out of it is the
dark path's progression, the way ranks at Sovern are humanity's.

**Human towns are hostile.** They are held by humans, and an undead is not welcome in any of them.
An undead player lives in undead territory or on neutral ground — Birkstein, Erdlis, Narimbia, High
Fall, Magnoria.

**So the trade is: easier to survive, harder to live.** Humanity has to eat and can walk anywhere.
The undead need nothing and are shut out of nearly everything.

### Undead politics

**The undead are split, and the split is the dark path's gameplay.**

- **Ambrose** wants humans dead.
- **Garskin** wants peace, and cannot be beaten by Ambrose.

**The Dark Lord is disengaged.** He does not care about politics. His two guardians run
everything between them, which means the entire faction is stable only because the strongest
thing in it would rather tend a garden.

**A player can play that politics** — instigating war, or preventing it, by moving high-ranking
undead (vampires, ghosts, monsters) to act. This is the undead's answer to humanity's map: they
hold five places and the means to take more.

**Ambrose is the youngest of the powers.** Garskin is 400 years old and wants a flower garden; the
Dark Lord is ancient and bored. Ambrose is barely a century dead and still burning. The war party
is led by the newest one there, because every old thing has let go and only the recent arrival
still hurts.

## Open questions

- **Does high undead have any pressure at all?** No hunger, no cold, no rot. Humanity's pressure
  is passive — weather, hunger, the year. The undead need an active one. The proposal on the table
  is that **the paladins at Mariacth hunt you**: you are not fighting the world, you are being
  looked for. Not settled.
- **Does the empty hotbar get replacements?** Either low undead carry embalming salt and grave
  dust while high undead simply carry less, or the emptier hotbar *is* the feeling. Not settled.
- **Does killing one gatekeeper close both directions, or only theirs?** Killing Ambrose plausibly
  ends human-to-undead conversion while leaving Theresa's cleansing intact. Not settled.
- **Humanity's peace faction.** The undead have Ambrose against Garskin. Humanity has corrupt
  paladins against nobody. The proposal is to make **Theresa** humanity's Garskin — the one who
  wants peace and knows what the paladins did — so that killing her is exactly as catastrophic,
  on the other side of the map. Not settled.

## Non-goals

- Elves. They are outside this entirely: the undead do not trouble them, they dislike the smell,
  and they are wary of humans over the forests. See `docs/cast/` for Maria.
