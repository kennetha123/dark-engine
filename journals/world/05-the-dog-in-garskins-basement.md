# Journal world/05 — The dog in Garskin's basement

Status: Decided.
Date: 2026-09-25. Depends on: `journals/world/02`, `journals/world/04`.

## Goal

Record Garskin precisely — what he is, what he holds up, and what killing him costs — because this
is the piece most likely to get softened later if it is not written down exactly.

## Decisions

**Garskin is a zombie with the highest physical strength in the world.** Higher than the Dark
Lord's. Ambrose, who is the greatest vampire that has ever existed, cannot beat him.

**He is nobody.** An unknown zombie. No title beyond second guardian, no lineage anybody records.

**He loves unconditionally, and he does not distinguish.** He gives money to whoever needs it and
helps whoever is in front of him. He does not see undead or human. He sees people, and treats them
alike.

**He wants a flower garden.** That is his ambition, entire.

**He watches sad plays.** He slips into the theatre at Tarniard — human territory — to sit in an
audience among people who would burn him if they knew. The strongest thing in the world takes that
risk to watch something that makes him cry. This gives Tarniard a purpose it did not have, and it
gives the player an encounter with Garskin that is not a battlefield.

### The dog

**There is a living dog in his basement.** One dog, hidden, four hundred years old.

**He met it in the first war between humans and undead**, four hundred years ago. It is a survivor
of the old times, as he is.

**He gave it undead power without the rot.** The dog does not decay, because **Garskin takes its
decay into himself.** Every ailment transfers to him. That is what it costs him, continuously, and
he has paid it for four centuries.

**The dog lives until Garskin is defeated.** The link is to Garskin's life, not to distance.

**Only the Dark Lord knows.** He has done nothing about it, because he cannot be bothered.

**Kill Garskin, then visit his house, and there is a dog in the basement, rotting.**

### What killing him actually does

The stability of the entire undead faction rests on him. Ambrose wants war and cannot get past
Garskin. So a humanity player who kills the hardest enemy in the game has, in one stroke:

- destroyed the undead's peace faction,
- handed Ambrose the field and started the war,
- and killed a dog.

**Every reward is a catastrophe.** He is not a boss. He is the thing holding the war shut.

### It has to be a choice

If the player could not have known, it is a trick, and a trick works once. The reveal lands after
the reward — that is correct — but the *information* must be available before the swing:

- **Rumours in the neutral towns** about the quiet one who does not want the war.
- **His flower garden**, visible before you reach him. Tended. Recently.
- **A dog barking from the basement during the fight.**

**The dog cannot be saved.** Take it out of the house first and kill him anyway — it still rots.
The only way the dog lives is if Garskin does.

## Open questions

- **The dog's name.** An unnamed dog is a prop. A name on a collar is a person, and the player
  reads it in the basement. Not chosen.
- **What Garskin does if the player is undead** and comes to him rather than to Ambrose. He is the
  peace faction; there should be a path through him.
- **Whether the garden survives him.** If the player kills him and comes back a season later, is
  it overgrown, or has somebody kept it?

## Non-goals

- Making him sympathetic through dialogue. He is sympathetic through what he does when nobody is
  watching. The player finds out by looking, not by being told.
